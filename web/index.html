          How routes are built

          Each vehicle's estimated facing plus the camera's look direction gives a compass heading (facing the camera = travelling toward it). A sighting is linked to a sighting at the next camera in that direction when the colour profile and vehicle class agree, the implied speed is 20–140 km/h, and no other candidate is nearly as good. Links are appearance-based guesses, not plate reads; white, black and grey vehicles need a closer match. Each refresh, the widget repeats the linking with every camera's clock shifted by 10–20 minutes. Links that survive that shift are coincidences, so the route list is only trusted when real links clearly outnumber them. Most DriveBC cameras refresh every few minutes, so on a typical corridor the same vehicle is rarely photographed twice and links are mostly chance. The direction-of-travel chart uses facing alone and does not depend on matching. Camera-to-camera routes come from sites where several views capture within seconds of each other. A vehicle approaching in one view appears moments later in another; it is matched on compass heading, colour and class within 30 s and 200 m, checked against shifted-clock decoys, and joined with same-camera tracks. Same-camera tracks follow one vehicle across consecutive frames of a camera by matching its appearance, position and size under an unchanged view. They are checked against a mirrored-frame decoy, and they reveal queues, lineups and slow traffic. Pick a corridor to refresh its cameras together, and keep this tab open to build history.

          Model & accuracy

          MobileNetV3 trained on the labeled CFV car dataset, then adapted to elevated DriveBC views with hand-labeled DriveBC crops. The crop and its mirror image are averaged. Confidence is shown as high, medium or low. Facing shows which side of the vehicle is visible; it does not measure movement.
