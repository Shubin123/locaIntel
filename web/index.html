          How tracking works

          Only relevant GPS is kept. While you log, or when you import a track, a fix is stored only if it is within 500 m of a DriveBC camera. The fix just before and just after each camera zone is kept too, within 30 s, so positions interpolate across the edge. Everything else is dropped before it reaches storage. Older full-length drives can be trimmed the same way.

          Camera matching. A pass is a visit within the match distance and in front of the camera's look direction. Your direction of travel against that look direction gives the side of the vehicle the camera should see.

          Time matching. A frame with an upstream capture time (DriveBC's Expires header, or a Replay the Day stamp) is in view when your interpolated position at that moment is. A frame with only a fetch time was captured up to the tolerance earlier, so it can only be a possible sighting. With capture on, cameras near you are re-fetched every 30 s and for 3 minutes after you leave: a capture made while you were in view stays the current image until the next one. DriveBC sees which camera images this device requests.

          Verification. Every detected vehicle in an in-view frame is scored against the profile. The cues are YOLO class against body type (hand-set compatibilities; there is no separate body-type classifier), colour (a saturation-weighted hue and brightness histogram, compared with your reference photos and confirmed sightings) and model facing against the expected side. Confirming needs a capture time, a colour or other appearance cue, and a clear margin over the next-best vehicle. White, grey and black count for less. A label is never used to score its own frame.

          Checks. The decoy rate re-runs verification on frames from the same cameras taken more than 10 minutes from any of your passes: confirmations there are strangers who look like your vehicle. Agreement is how often the automatic pick matches the frames you labelled. DriveBC keeps about 24 hours of images per camera. python -m pipeline.gps_replay downloads the frames nearest each pass from the export; import that folder with Import replay frames. Each vehicle is followed from its own GPS track; nothing here searches cameras for a vehicle without one.

                  How routes are built

                  Each vehicle's estimated facing plus the camera's look direction gives a compass heading (facing the camera = travelling toward it). A sighting is linked to a sighting at the next camera in that direction when the colour profile and vehicle class agree, the implied speed is 20–140 km/h, and no other candidate is nearly as good. Links are appearance-based guesses, not plate reads; white, black and grey vehicles need a closer match. Each refresh, the panel repeats the linking with every camera's clock shifted by 10–20 minutes. Links that survive that shift are coincidences, so the route list is only trusted when real links clearly outnumber them. Most DriveBC cameras refresh every few minutes, so on a typical corridor the same vehicle is rarely photographed twice and links are mostly chance. The direction-of-travel chart and the map's Facing layer use facing alone and do not depend on matching. Camera-to-camera routes come from sites where several views capture within seconds of each other. A vehicle approaching in one view appears moments later in another; it is matched on compass heading, colour and class within 30 s and 200 m, checked against shifted-clock decoys, and joined with same-camera tracks. Same-camera tracks follow one vehicle across consecutive frames of a camera by matching its appearance, position and size under an unchanged view. They are checked against a mirrored-frame decoy, and they reveal queues, lineups and slow traffic. Pick a corridor to refresh its cameras together, and keep this tab open to build history.
